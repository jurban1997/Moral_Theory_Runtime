## 0. Strategic Reframing (adopted 2026-07-17)

Two strategic decisions, made before executing the per-item mitigations, change how the plan below is read. Where a workstream entry conflicts with this section, this section controls.

### 0.1 The axiom is the index of a constitutional cluster, not a self-contained statement

**Decision.** The axiom does not stand entirely on its own. Its terms and relationships are clarified in named supporting sections (Foundational Definitions, Operational Primitives, the Normative Kernel, the boundary-selection protocol), and those clarifications share the axiom's constitutional status where the W3 charter says so. The statement stays concise and comprehensible; the operative content lives where there is room to be precise.

**Precedent and internal evidence.** Statutes carry definitions sections; Rawls's principles carry priority rules; constitutional articles interpret one another. The framework already practices this implicitly: Normative Kernel item 4 extends the material-diminishment constraint to "Flourishing Entities **or Moral Patients**," which exceeds the axiom's literal protected class (C9). Formalizing the cluster legitimizes existing practice.

**Consequences for the plan.**

- **W4 shrinks to near zero text change.** FA-8, FA-9, FA-11, and FA-12 are reclassified from "axiom text defects" to "missing constitutional supporting content." They are mitigated by developing the supporting sections and binding them to the axiom through the charter — not by amending the statement. This avoids wording churn against the Example Assessments and avoids spurious "new variant" identity triggers. FA-7's conflict clause becomes a constitutional corollary adjacent to the axiom rather than an amendment of it.
- **W3 becomes the centerpiece.** The cluster strategy answers FA-3 only if supporting-section content carries the same fixity as the statement; otherwise the "concise axiom" is a facade and the Pragmatist objection worsens. The charter is the instrument that prevents this: it enumerates which propositions in which supporting sections are constitutionally fixed.
- **Guardrails.** (1) *Dispersal risk:* the cluster must be explicitly enumerable — §4 should state, in one sentence, that the axiom is to be read with its named supporting sections, and the charter should list them. (2) *Humpty-Dumpty risk:* supporting definitions clarify terms; they may not redefine plain language to smuggle in scope changes. FA-13/FA-14/FA-15 therefore remain genuine normative decisions (W5), not drafting maneuvers.

### 0.2 The flourishing circularity is mutual constitution: ground the recursion instead of breaking it

**Decision.** FA-6 is reframed. The interdependence in the definition — an entity flourishes optimally only within a flourishing context (culture, environment, ecosystem), and the entity has agency to improve the flourishing of that context — is not a definitional defect but a structural property of flourishing itself. The mitigation changes from *eliminating* the circularity to making the recursion *well-founded*:

1. **Base:** entity-intrinsic dimensions (persistence, adaptive capacity, future possibilities) defined and measurable without invoking flourishing;
2. **Coupling relations:** an explicit context-support relation (how context conditions enable member flourishing) and contribution relation (how member conduct affects context flourishing — the formal home of Constructive Participation);
3. **Feedback loop:** agency as the mechanism by which entities improve the context that in turn enables them — which is what axiom clause C6 ("fostering the adaptive development of moral agents") has been trying to say. This settles Open Decision 5 in favor of **developing C6 in place**;
4. **Non-vacuity demonstrations:** the definition must discriminate cases — viral expansion must fail the contribution relation, and oppressive stability must fail the member-support relation — or the reframing is only a more elegant circle.

This structure is a fixed-point definition of the kind used in systems theory, ecology, and economics, and the autopoiesis literature the framework already cites is built on exactly it.

**Consequences for the plan.**

- **FA-6:** mitigation is now "specify base + coupling relations + non-vacuity demonstrations" (updated in W5). The RQ-3 deliverable changes shape accordingly.
- **FA-10 largely dissolves:** a context that persists by suppressing member flourishing fails the member-support coupling; the anti-oppressive-stability test falls out of the structure rather than requiring a bolt-on gloss. ~~Residual W4 action: a brief clarifying note, not a text change.~~ *Superseded: the author directed a C1 text change (§1.10), which resolves the remaining half of FA-10 — the "stable" in "stable moral system" now unambiguously describes the moral system's own evaluative reliability, not the value of stability in anything it assesses.*
- **FA-11 is resolved in direction:** C6 is the feedback loop of the coupled system; develop it operationally via Distributed Policy Updates machinery (§6.9).
- **FA-16 / W1 gain a convergence exhibit:** Aristotelian polis-dependence of eudaimonia, Confucian relational personhood, care ethics' relational ontology, and capability conversion factors each independently assert the entity–context interdependence. Cross-tradition convergence on the coupled structure is affirmative evidence for the construct, converting the circularity objection into support for the defense dossier.

**Unchanged by the reframing.** FA-1, FA-2, FA-4, FA-5 (authority, legitimacy, amendment, kernel derivation) still require the W1–W3 work: a concise axiom with a constitutional cluster still needs defending, governing, and mapping. FA-13, FA-14, FA-15 still require explicit normative decisions about the protected class.

## 1. Original Statement and Role

The original (v1) axiom, as it stood in `framework.md` §4.1 until superseded by the adopted v2.4 text (§1.4) on 2026-09-27. It is retained here because the weakness catalog (§2) was written against it and because `framework.md` §4.7 preserves it as the amendment record:

> "A stable moral system preserves and promotes the long-term flourishing of relevant persistent adaptive entities—including autopoietic entities—by fostering the adaptive development of moral agents while avoiding actions that, based on the best available evidence within the Situational Context, materially diminish the long-term flourishing of other relevant persistent adaptive entities."

`framework.md` assigns the axiom three roles: it supplies the normative direction the Reference Architecture operationalizes; it is "constitutional" within the Normative Kernel (§5 — replacing it "ordinarily defines a different framework variant"); and it is offered as the framework's answer to the is–ought gap ("every moral framework requires at least one foundational assumption," made explicit rather than hidden).

| C2 | "preserves and promotes" | The positive duty (direction of evaluation) |

| C4 | "of relevant" | The scope filter on who counts in an assessment |

Every other clause reference in this document applies unchanged.

## 1.4 Adopted Axiom — Draft v2.4 (Full Assembled Text)

> **This is the adopted axiom, applied to `framework.md` §4.1 on 2026-09-27.** Sections 1.5–1.10 below are the change history: each records only the delta it introduced (v2 baseline → v2.1 sentence-3 revision → v2.2 nested-context insert → v2.3 insert revision → v2.4 primary-object term and context-tier ordering → v2.4 C1 strengthening and adoption). This section assembles them into the complete text. When a new delta is adopted, update this section and `framework.md` §4.1 together.

> "A stable moral system — one that applies across every domain of action, extends to new entities and circumstances without loss of identity, and whose judgments, resting on explicit evidence and reasoning rather than on the standpoint of any assessor, withstand challenge from every standpoint — preserves and promotes the long-term flourishing of relevant Flourishing Entities: persistent adaptive entities, including autopoietic entities, whose long-term flourishing can rise or fall. The flourishing of any Flourishing Entity and the flourishing of the contexts in which it participates are mutually constitutive and recursive: an entity flourishes fully only within a flourishing context, and the entity's own conduct sustains or degrades the flourishing of that context. These contexts occupy three tiers — the Biosphere, Society, and the Individual — and within each tier they overlap and nest with one another, from the immediate situation of each Individual, through the families, institutions, and cultures that compose Society, to the ecosystems that compose the Biosphere, forming a connected lattice of dependency. The flourishing of any broader context is constituted by the capacity of the contexts and Flourishing Entities within it to flourish, sustained across the characteristic time horizons over which each persists and renews itself; and each narrower context draws its enabling conditions from the flourishing of those that contain it. Because enablement runs downward, the tiers are ordered in priority — the Biosphere before Society, and Society before the Individual — so that where the sustained capacities of contexts at different tiers genuinely conflict, the broader tier prevails, since its diminishment propagates to everything it enables. This priority orders enabling conditions, not moral worth: the flourishing of the whole can never be secured by systematically diminishing the sustained capacity of its parts, and no tier's priority by itself justifies a material diminishment that this axiom otherwise forbids. A moral system therefore fosters the adaptive development of moral agents — a constituent of their own flourishing, not merely a means — strengthening their capacity and disposition to improve the contexts, at every tier, that enable their own flourishing and the flourishing of others. In pursuing these ends, a moral system avoids actions that, based on the best available evidence within the Situational Context, materially diminish the long-term flourishing of other relevant Flourishing Entities — recognizing that every action transforms the Biosphere, Society, and Individuals that all future flourishing, and every future assessment, inherit."

## 1.5 Candidate Axiom Restatement — Draft v2 (2026-07-17)

> **Superseded as text by §1.4 (Draft v2.4).** Retained as the v2 baseline and clause-mapping record; the current sentence-by-clause map is in §1.4.

Per the directive that the axiom explicitly reference the recursiveness identified in §0.2, the following multi-sentence restatement is proposed. It is designed as a **clarifying amendment** under the W2 taxonomy: every operative clause of the current statement (C1–C9) is preserved, with C6's original wording retained verbatim; the additions make explicit what the Long-Term Flourishing definition ("compatible with, and must contribute to") already implied.

> "A stable moral system preserves and promotes the long-term flourishing of relevant persistent adaptive entities, including autopoietic entities. The flourishing of any such entity and the flourishing of the contexts in which it participates — cultures, institutions, ecosystems, and the broader World Context — are mutually constitutive and recursive: an entity flourishes fully only within a flourishing context, and the entity's own conduct sustains or degrades the flourishing of that context. A moral system therefore fosters the adaptive development of moral agents, strengthening their capacity and disposition to improve the contexts that enable their own flourishing and the flourishing of others. In pursuing these ends, a moral system avoids actions that, based on the best available evidence within the Situational Context, materially diminish the long-term flourishing of other relevant persistent adaptive entities — recognizing that every action transforms the World Context that all future flourishing, and every future assessment, inherits."

## 1.6 Regression of Draft v2 Against the Traditional Ethical Frameworks (2026-07-17)

Each of the 14 traditions cataloged in `framework.md` §9.2/§9.3 was re-run against Draft v2: does the tradition's counterargument land harder, softer, or unchanged, and do the new clauses (R1 mutual constitution, R2 feedback purpose, R3 temporal recursion) create new attack surface?

### Tradition-by-Tradition Results

| Tradition | Effect of Draft v2 | Assessment |
|-----------|-------------------|------------|
| Utilitarianism | Unchanged core; new ambiguity | The decision-rule objection is untouched (deliberately deferred to Phase 6). New question surfaced: is "context flourishing" independently valuable or just aggregate member flourishing restated? If independent, holism must be defended; if not, R1 is decorative. → **SW-1** |
| Deontology (Kant, Ross) | **New exposure** | R1 textually elevates collective/context health while the individual-protecting brake (materiality τ, rights layer) remains unformalized — the "sacrifice the individual for the flourishing context" reading is *stronger* in v2 than in v1 until Phase 4 completes. → **SW-2**. | Rights-Based Ethics | Same as Deontology | Rights exist precisely to trump collective goods; SW-2 applies identically. No additional distinct exposure. |
| Virtue Ethics | **Improved** | R1 is the polis-dependence of eudaimonia made explicit; R2's "capacity and disposition" is virtue-adjacent language and gives the cultivation insight constitutional standing. Residual: "adaptive development" still is not "virtue" — unchanged from v1. |

| Cosmism | Slightly improved | R3's temporal recursion is congenial to long-horizon obligations. No new exposure. |
| Pragmatism | **New exposure** | Two issues: (a) the fixed core grows by three propositions, scaling up the unrevisability objection; (b) R1 is part-empirical ("entities in fact flourish only in flourishing contexts") and part-normative — embedding a quasi-empirical thesis in the constitutional layer creates an *empirical hostage*: counterevidence (entities that flourish despite degraded contexts) would impeach the axiom itself. → **SW-5** |
| Contractarianism | Slightly improved | R1 provides mutual-advantage/common-good material usable in public justification (W2). The legitimacy gap itself (FA-2) is unchanged. SW-2's collectivist tilt noted but the contractarian version adds nothing beyond the deontological one. |
| Natural Law | **Improved** | R1 aligns with the common-good doctrine (bonum commune): the person flourishes in community. The basic-goods and categorical-constraint gaps are unchanged. |
| Confucian Ethics | **Improved** | Strongest convergence gain: R1 is relational personhood; R2 is cultivation and governance-by-example given constitutional status. Residual computation-vs-cultivation worry unchanged. |
| Systems Ethics | Improved + **new exposure** | R1/R3 are systems-native (coupling, feedback, recursion) and the oppressive-stability case now fails the axiom's own text (helps FA-10/F-4). But R1 opens a new boundary-gaming vector: **context individuation**. Which contexts count, who defines them, and how do nested or conflicting contexts (family vs. firm vs. nation vs. biosphere) trade off? An actor can select the context whose flourishing their action serves ("my institution flourishes"). This multiplies FA-7's conflict silence across context levels. → **SW-4** |
| Capability Approach | **Improved** | The context-support relation is conversion factors made constitutional: nominal opportunity without contextual support is not full flourishing. Threshold and weighting gaps unchanged. |
| Environmental / Deep Ecology | Mixed + **new exposure** | Ecosystems now appear in the axiom — but as *contexts* (enabling conditions for members) rather than as *entities with their own standing*. A deep ecologist will read this as demoting nature to backdrop, and the wording risks prejudging the pending W5 ecological-standing decision (FA-14) toward instrumentalism. The context/entity status of ecosystems must be explicitly both/and. → **SW-8** |

## 1.7 Nested Context Hierarchy — Assessment and Candidate Draft v2.2 (2026-07-17)

**Proposal assessed:** contexts form a connected, dependent hierarchy — World Context flourishing requires Local Context flourishing, which requires Situational Context flourishing — and this dependency linkage belongs in the axiom, potentially also structuring context individuation (SW-4) by scope.

### Verdict: sound in core, adopted with two corrections and one category caution

**Sound core.** The proposal extends R1's one-level recursion (entity ↔ context) to the multi-level structure reality actually has, with two complementary directions: *upward constitution* (a broader context's flourishing is built from the states of its constituent scopes) and *downward enablement* (each narrower scope draws its possibilities from the health of the scopes containing it — already in R1). The structure has independent support in systems theory (holarchy), ecology (panarchy — nested adaptive cycles), and developmental psychology (Bronfenbrenner's ecological model), adding to the W1 convergence exhibit.

**Key protective payoff.** Stated as constitution, the dependency linkage is *structurally anti-collectivist*: if the whole's flourishing is made of the capacity of its parts, the whole can never be purchased by sacrificing the parts — the "greater good" argument becomes incoherent at the axiom level rather than merely threshold-constrained. This directly counters **SW-2** and gives the AI-eradication example (§8) an axiom-level answer: an objective framed as "planetary stability" self-refutes if it destroys the constituent scopes whose capacity planetary flourishing is constituted by.

**Correction 1 — capacity, not instance.** The strict universal reading (every situational context must flourish for the World Context to flourish) is false: forest fires serve ecosystem renewal, firm failure serves economic health, apoptosis serves the organism; panarchy shows small-scale collapse-and-renewal is often constitutive of large-scale resilience. The strict version also makes whole-flourishing vacuously unattainable. Sound formulation: **a broader context's flourishing requires the sustained *capacity* of its narrower contexts to flourish** (preservation of beneficial state space at each scope — connecting to BFSS and the Adaptive Capacity / Future Possibilities dimensions), not the momentary flourishing of every occupant. The anti-sacrifice protection survives in the correct form: what can never serve the whole is *systematic or irreversible destruction of a constituent scope's capacity*.

**Correction 2 — hierarchy, not chain.** Contexts nest vertically but also overlap horizontally (family, firm, culture, ecosystem are not nested in each other); the real structure is a lattice. The hierarchy therefore resolves the *vertical* half of **SW-4**: (a) individuation discipline — any claimed context must be located at a scope level with its dependency links declared, blocking free-floating context selection ("my institution flourishes"); (b) a principled weighting seed for Phase 6 — harm propagating down the enablement chain (climate, institutional trust, epistemic commons) is structurally weightier than contained harm. Horizontal sibling-context conflicts remain routed to the Phase 6 conflict-resolution procedure. SW-4's disposition is updated accordingly (partially resolved at the constitutional level; residual scope in RQ-6/RQ-7).

**Category caution.** In the current architecture the World → Local → Situational stack is *epistemic* (filters defining what an assessment must consider), while this argument concerns *ontological* nesting (situations, communities, ecosystems as real systems). The axiom text should therefore express the dependency over ontological scope levels in plain language, not the technical Stack terms; the supporting sections should then declare the Context Stack to be the epistemic mirror of the ontological nesting. Side benefit: this gives the Context Stack a principled justification it currently lacks — it tracks real structure rather than being a modeling convenience.

### Candidate Draft v2.2

Insert after the mutual-constitution sentence (sentence 2 of v2.1); all other sentences unchanged:

The flourishing of the whole can therefore never be secured by systematically diminishing the capacity of its parts."

**Clause registry additions:** R4 (nested constitution — broader-scope flourishing is constituted by constituent scopes' sustained capacity), R5 (downward enablement across scopes), R6 (anti-sacrifice corollary — whole-flourishing cannot be secured by systematic diminishment of constituent capacity). ## 1.8 "Sustained" Grounded in Time-Scale + Lattice Model — Candidate Draft v2.3 (2026-07-17)

Two defects in Draft v2.2 identified and corrected:

1. **"Sustained" was asserted, not grounded.** v2.2 used "sustained capacity" without saying what sustains it or over what. The word now receives explicit temporal content, providing purchase for time-scale measurement.
2. **"Hierarchy" contradicted the analysis.** §1.7's own Correction 2 established that contexts form a lattice (vertical nesting plus horizontal overlap), yet the v2.2 text said "connected and dependent hierarchy." The text now says what the analysis concluded.

### Grounding "Sustained": Characteristic Time Horizons

Each lattice level has a characteristic tempo (panarchy's nested adaptive cycles): situations play out over minutes–days; institutions over years–decades; cultures over generations; ecosystems over centuries; the World Context over civilizational spans. **"Sustained" means maintained or renewed across the characteristic horizons over which that context persists.**

Measurement consequences:

- Capacity is assessed as **level and trend** at each materially relevant horizon: a capacity intact at assessment time but undergoing uncompensated erosion is *not* sustained (the erosion test).
- Renewal cycles are accommodated: collapse-and-renewal at a fast level (fire, bankruptcy, apoptosis) is consistent with sustained capacity at that level provided renewal mechanisms remain intact — connecting directly to §1.7's Correction 1.
- The framework's Assessment Horizon set (immediate → civilizational) acquires a structural rationale it previously lacked: the horizons track the characteristic tempos of the context lattice's levels, rather than being an arbitrary enumeration. Horizon selection for any assessment should match the tempos of the contexts materially affected.

**Charter provision (fixed content):** *sustained capacity* = capacity maintained or renewed across the characteristic time horizons of the context in question, where uncompensated erosion of capacity or of renewal mechanisms defeats the "sustained" qualifier even when present levels are adequate. The specific tempo assignments per context class are interpretable, evidence-revisable parameters.

### Lattice Model Adopted in Text

The lattice replaces "hierarchy" in the statement. Additional payoff for **SW-4** beyond §1.7's vertical individuation: overlapping sibling contexts have a **meet** (shared constituent scopes — common members, resources, conditions) and a **join** (smallest context containing both). Sibling-context conflicts — the residual SW-4 case the pure hierarchy could not address — acquire a principled evaluation frame: assess the action's effect on the sustained capacity of the meet and of the join. This does not replace the Phase 6 conflict-resolution procedure but supplies the structure it operates over. SW-4's disposition is upgraded accordingly (vertical individuation resolved in §1.7; horizontal conflicts now structurally framed, with resolution rules remaining in RQ-6/RQ-7).

## 1.9 Primary Object Term and Context-Tier Ordering — Candidate Draft v2.4 (2026-09-27)

Two author directives are executed in this delta, and the Systems Ethics ambiguity (SW-4) is answered directly rather than routed onward.

### Directive 1 — "Flourishing Entities" is the primary object; "persistent adaptive entities" is its definition

**Change.** Wherever the statement names the object of the positive duty (C5) or the protected class of the side-constraint (C9), it now uses the framework's defined term **Flourishing Entities**. The former phrase is retained once, as the term's definition, in an appositive on first use: *"relevant Flourishing Entities — persistent adaptive entities, including autopoietic entities, whose long-term flourishing can rise or fall."*

**Why this is an alignment gain, not just a rename.** `framework.md` already fragments the axiom's protected class across its own restatements: the moral-system objective (§5.6 *Foundational or Moral-System Objectives*) uses "Flourishing Entities" on both sides of the duty; the workflow step *Evaluate Against the Foundational Axiom* (§6.1) and *World Context Transition and the Foundational Axiom* (§6.10) use "Flourishing Entities" for the positive duty but "persistent adaptive entities" for the side-constraint; kernel item 4 and the Material Diminishment definition use "Flourishing Entities or Moral Patients." The axiom was the only place still using the undefined phrase as the primary object. Converging the statement on the defined term removes three-way class drift and makes C5/C9 traceable to a single §3 definition.

**Definitional consequence (adoption condition).** `framework.md` §3 currently defines Flourishing Entity as a *Self-Directed Entity* with measurable LTF, where Self-Directed requires an internal policy (`SelfDirected := Autopoietic ∧ InternalPolicy ≠ ∅`). The directive re-grounds the term on **persistence and adaptive capacity**: a Flourishing Entity is a persistent adaptive entity — including, but not limited to, autopoietic and self-directed entities — whose long-term flourishing can be meaningfully increased or diminished. Formal shape: `FlourishingEntity(o) := Persistent(o) ∧ Adaptive(o) ∧ Measurable(LTF(o))`, with `Autopoietic(o)` and `SelfDirected(o)` recorded as sufficient (not necessary) grounds for `Adaptive(o)`. Two co-requisites: (a) `Persistent` and `Adaptive` must be defined as §0.2 base dimensions — measurable *without* invoking flourishing — which requires rewording `framework.md`'s Adaptive Capacity definition (§3 *Adaptive Capacity*, Architectural: "the ability of a *Flourishing Entity* to…") so that it does not presuppose the predicate it now grounds; (b) the entity ladder (Object → Autopoietic → Self-Directed → Flourishing Entity) becomes a set of sufficient-condition paths rather than a strict chain.

**Effects on the catalog.**

- **C-2 / FA-13 (partial):** the documented inconsistency — that low-agency entities cannot be Flourishing Entities because the predicate requires self-direction — dissolves, because persistence and adaptation do not require an internal policy. The *sentience* question (whether suffering is an independent standing ground) remains Open Decision 2; the disjunctive criterion is still recommended, now reading "Flourishing Entity **or** sentient being **or** classified Moral Patient."
- **FA-14 / Open Decision 3 (track 1 follows by definition):** ecological wholes that exhibit persistence and adaptive (regenerative) organization satisfy the re-grounded predicate directly, so the "ecological wholes as entities" track no longer needs a separate standing decision. Track 2 (abiotic systems as `ValueBearingProcesses`) remains open. This is a substantive widening of the protected class and is one reason v2.4 is classed as a normative amendment (below).
- **SW-1 / SW-8 (strengthened):** the both/and charter declaration now has textual support — an ecosystem is a context in the Biosphere tier *and* a Flourishing Entity when it satisfies the predicate. Sentence 4's "contexts and Flourishing Entities within it" makes the dual status explicit.
- **§0.1 Humpty-Dumpty guardrail:** this change *is* a scope change effected through a definition, which the guardrail exists to catch. It is permitted here because it is being made as a declared normative decision by the author and recorded as such, not as a drafting maneuver; the W2 procedure applies.

### Directive 2 — Three context tiers in descending priority: Biosphere, Society, Individual

**Change.** The v2.3 lattice is retained but organized into three ordered **tiers**. Sentence 3 places the lattice's contexts by tier (ecosystems → Biosphere; families, institutions, cultures → Society; the immediate situation of each Individual → Individual). Sentence 5 (new **R8**) states the ordering and its ground: *because enablement runs downward, the tiers are ordered in priority — the Biosphere before Society, and Society before the Individual — so that where the sustained capacities of contexts at different tiers genuinely conflict, the broader tier prevails, since its diminishment propagates to everything it enables.*

**Reading adopted.** "Importance or priority" is read as **priority in genuine conflict, grounded in enabling dependency** — not as a ranking of intrinsic moral worth. This is the reading §1.7 Correction 2 already identified as the "principled weighting seed for Phase 6" (harm propagating down the enablement chain is structurally weightier than contained harm); v2.4 promotes it from supporting-section seed to constitutional clause. Sentence 6 states the reading in the text ("this priority orders enabling conditions, not moral worth") so that it cannot be lost in the charter. If the author intends the stronger reading — intrinsic precedence of the whole over its members — that is a different commitment with the SW-2/SW-10 exposures below unmitigated, and it should be recorded as a further decision (Open Decision 7).

**Interaction with R6 (anti-sacrifice) — the load-bearing reconciliation.** A priority ordering and an anti-sacrifice corollary look contradictory: if the Biosphere prevails over the Individual, is the Individual not sacrificed? The two clauses govern different things:

- **R8b** governs *which tier's sustained capacity prevails* when an action's effects on the sustained capacities of contexts at different tiers genuinely conflict. It supplies the ordering that the Material Diminishment primitive's tragic-choice comparison (severity / necessity / distribution) otherwise lacks.
- **R6 + R8c** govern *accounting and permission*: systematic diminishment of a constituent scope's sustained capacity cannot be counted as securing the broader tier's flourishing (the SW-9 constitutive reading), and tier priority never *by itself* justifies a material diminishment — the necessity, proportionality, and least-damage test and the actor's burden of proof (FA-8) still apply in full.

So the ordering decides *whose capacity is weightier*; it does not waive the side-constraint. An action that materially diminishes Individuals for a Society- or Biosphere-tier reason must still be necessary, least-damaging, and proportionate, and must actually protect the broader tier's *sustained capacity* — not its convenience, preference, or a single institution's interests (SW-11).

**Why Biosphere is the apex term.** "Biosphere" replaces "World Context" everywhere in the statement (sentences 2, 3, 8). This executes §1.7's category caution — the statement now speaks only in plain-language ontological scopes — and resolves the tension flagged in the 2026-09-27 alignment review that v2.3 used the technical Context Stack term as the top of an ontological lattice. The charter declares the Context Stack's *World Context* to be the epistemic mirror of the three-tier ontology (the state container in which Biosphere, Society, and Individuals are represented), which gives the Stack the principled justification §1.7 anticipated.

### Direct Response to the Systems Ethics Ambiguity (SW-4)

The Systems Ethics regression (§1.6) surfaced a three-part ambiguity: (i) *which contexts count*, (ii) *who defines the context set*, and (iii) *how nested or conflicting contexts trade off* — with the practical exposure that an actor selects the context whose flourishing their action serves ("my institution flourishes"). §1.7 answered the vertical half (scope location, dependency declaration) and §1.8 supplied a frame for sibling conflicts (meet/join) while leaving the resolution rule to Phase 6. v2.4 answers all three parts at the constitutional level:

1. **Which contexts count.** Every context materially affected by an action is located in exactly one of three exhaustive tiers — Biosphere, Society, Individual — and, within its tier, in the lattice of overlapping and nested contexts. A context that cannot be placed in a tier with its dependency links declared is not an admissible context for the assessment. There is no fourth, free-floating tier in which a favorable context can be constructed.

2. **Who defines the context set.** Not the actor. The assessment must declare the affected contexts *at every tier*, with an exclusion log for contexts considered and omitted, under the Assessment Boundary Selection protocol (adoption condition 12 below). Because all three tiers must be populated, the "select the context my action serves" move is blocked structurally: the actor's preferred context is assessed alongside every other affected context, not instead of them.

3. **How conflicts trade off.** Two rules, one per conflict type:
   - *Cross-tier conflicts* (Individual vs. Society, Society vs. Biosphere) are ordered by **R8b** — the broader tier's sustained capacity prevails — subject to **R6/R8c** (constitutive accounting; no waiver of the side-constraint; necessity/least-damage test).
   - *Within-tier (sibling) conflicts* (family vs. firm; one ecosystem vs. another) are evaluated over the **meet and join** of the conflicting contexts (§1.8): assess the action's effect on the sustained capacity of what the siblings share and of the smallest context containing both. The residual — a ranking rule *among* siblings when meet/join analysis does not decide — remains with Phase 6 / RQ-6, but it is now the only piece of SW-4 that does.

**Boundary-gaming vectors that remain, and their treatment.** The tier structure closes free-floating context selection but opens two narrower vectors, cataloged as SW-11 and SW-12 below: *tier inflation* (presenting one institution's interests as "Society's" sustained capacity) and *tier weaponization* (invoking Biosphere priority to justify catastrophic diminishment of Society or Individuals — the ecofascist reading). Both are answered by the same distinction the statement already draws: priority attaches to a *tier's sustained capacity*, not to any context's or entity's interests, and R6 forbids counting the destruction of constituents as the flourishing of the whole.

**SW-4 disposition (updated):** *Substantially resolved at the constitutional level by R8 (v2.4)* — individuation (which contexts, declared by whom) and cross-tier trade-off are fixed in the statement; sibling-ranking rules remain with Phase 6 / RQ-6–RQ-7 and are the only residual.

## 1.10 C1 Strengthening and Adoption of Draft v2.4 (2026-09-27)

### Directive — strengthen "A stable moral system" (C1)

**Change.** C1 now reads: *"A stable moral system — one that applies across every domain of action, extends to new entities and circumstances without loss of identity, and whose judgments, resting on explicit evidence and reasoning rather than on the standpoint of any assessor, withstand challenge from every standpoint —"*. The four properties named are the ones a well-defined system should have: stability (of evaluative behavior), broad applicability, extensibility, and objective defensibility.

**How "objective unassailability" is rendered.** The directive's phrase is carried by *"whose judgments, resting on explicit evidence and reasoning rather than on the standpoint of any assessor, withstand challenge from every standpoint."* It is deliberately a claim about the system's *judgments given its declared inputs* — standpoint-invariance — and not a claim that the axiom itself is immune from challenge. Three internal constraints force that rendering: kernel item 3 defines objective assessment as "explicit and auditable reasoning, not value-neutrality"; §4.6 retains "the axiom remains an explicit assumption"; and §9.4.1 prohibits "claims of moral objectivity unsupported by the framework." A literal "unassailable" would contradict all three and hand Pragmatism (SW-5, FA-3) a decisive objection. The reading rule in `framework.md` §4.7 states the rendering. *If the author intends the stronger claim, it should be recorded as a decision with those three passages revised to match.*

**Consistency review of C1's application.** "Stable moral system" occurred in `framework.md` only in the axiom statement; every restatement of the axiom (§5.1, §5.6, §6.1, §6.10, §8, Abstract) paraphrases the duties without characterizing the system, so no restatement conflicted. The four properties correspond to existing Design Principles (§2 *Extensibility*, *Objective Assessment*, *Explicit Assumptions*, *Technology Independence*) and to three bullets of §4.5 *Why This Axiom* ("explicit," "can be applied to biological, social, institutional, and artificial systems," "computationally representable"); §4.5 now also carries a bullet stating the properties, so the axiom and its rationale say the same thing. Two consequences were checked: (a) C1 characterizes the *assessing* system, so FA-10's residual reading — that "stable" values stability in *assessed* systems — is closed by text, and §4.7 says expressly that a persistent harmful system carries no positive weight; (b) FA-1's criticism (design virtues are not reasons for content) is not answered by C1 and is not made worse by it — the properties are now claimed *by* the axiom, which makes them testable conformance properties, but the defense of the axiom's *content* remains the W1 dossier.

**FA-10 disposition:** resolved by text (C1) plus structure (R1 member-support coupling); the three inconsistent dispositions noted in the 2026-09-27 alignment review (§0.2 "dissolves," W4 "replace or gloss," v2.3 "unchanged") are reconciled — W4's entry is marked resolved and §0.2's note updated.

## 3. Mitigation Plan

Each item receives: the mitigation, the concrete deliverable, dependencies, and an acceptance criterion. Items are grouped into five workstreams (W1–W5) that can be executed largely in the order listed; W1–W3 fall under Phase 1 of the Remediation Sequence, W4–W5 under Phase 2 (they feed RQ-2/RQ-3 but are triggered by axiom text decisions).

### W1 — Axiom Defense Dossier *(mitigates FA-1, FA-16; Phase 1 / RQ-1)*

| Item | Mitigation Summary |
|------|--------------------|
| FA-1 | Write a dedicated **defense section** for §4 that argues *for* the axiom's content rather than its format, using three mutually reinforcing strategies: (1) a **reflective-equilibrium argument** — show the axiom reproduces considered judgments across the eight Example Assessments better than rival axioms; (2) a **convergence argument** — show that flourishing-preservation is an overlapping-consensus point that utilitarian welfare, capability thresholds, natural-law basic goods, and care-ethical dependency claims each partially endorse (turning §9.2's comparisons from defensive to probative); (3) an **error-theory of rivals** — state precisely which considered judgments each rival axiom fails (e.g., utility aggregation fails concentrated-harm cases). Acceptance: reviewers from three traditions can identify which of the three arguments answers their tradition's challenge, and what would count as defeating it. |
| FA-16 | Within the dossier, add an explicit **"contested evaluand" subsection** that takes positions instead of labeling disagreements: (a) take the deontological fork deliberately — recommend adopting a small set of kernel-level categorical constraints as *independent normative commitments* (not derived from flourishing), with the honesty cost that the framework becomes explicitly pluralist at its foundation; (b) answer Stoicism/Virtue by adopting the two-place solution the framework already sketches — agent-quality and transition-quality as separate axes — and *arguing* (not stipulating) why a systems ethic must keep external effects evaluable. ### W2 — Legitimacy, Adoption, and Amendment *(mitigates FA-2, FA-4; Phase 1 / RQ-1)*

| Item | Mitigation Summary |
|------|--------------------|
| FA-2 | Add a **legitimacy model** distinguishing three adoption modes with different consent requirements: (1) *voluntary individual use* (no consent problem); (2) *institutional adoption* (legitimacy inherited from the institution's own authority processes — board, legislature, regulator — which must be documented in the Authority Matrix); (3) *embedded deployment in systems affecting non-consenting parties* (the hard case: require public justification, contestability mechanisms, appeal rights, and exit options as *conformance conditions* for such deployments, so legitimacy is procedural and ongoing rather than assumed).
