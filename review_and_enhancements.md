# Review and Enhancements — Objective-Based Systems Ethics (framework.md)

> **Relocation (2026-09-28).** The outline this file cites has been deconstructed. The published entry point is [Objective-Based_Systems_Ethics.md](Objective-Based_Systems_Ethics.md). Section numbers (§) still refer to that outline; the master bibliography maps them to the new documents. Line numbers refer to the archived source, [Archive/framework.md](Archive/framework.md).


**Purpose.** This document inventories the weaknesses in `framework.md` as exposed by the arguments and counter-arguments of other ethical frameworks. Each weakness is stated as a specific, addressable deficiency, attributed to the source framework(s) whose critique generates the insight, and located in the document. The rightmost column (**Suggested Enhancement**) is intentionally left blank; it will be populated in a later step.

**Method.** The review assessed (a) the framework's own core content (Foundational Axiom, Foundational Definitions, Normative Kernel and Ethical Profiles, Operational Primitives, Moral Assessment Methodology, Assessment Output); (b) §9.2 *Comparison with Existing Ethical Frameworks* (14 traditions, ~lines 11709–18542); (c) §9.3 *Anticipated Counterarguments and Responses* (14 tradition-based objections, ~lines 18542–21792); and (d) §9.4 *Known Limitations* and §10 *Open Research Questions* (~lines 21792–24575). Line numbers cited below are approximate and refer to the current revision of `framework.md`.

**How to read the tables.**

- **Severity** — *Critical*: undermines the framework's central claims or usability if unaddressed. *High*: a major gap that a competent reviewer from the source tradition would treat as disqualifying until fixed. *Medium*: substantive but containable. *Low*: editorial or presentational.
- **Current Status in Document** — how `framework.md` itself treats the weakness today: *Conceded–deferred* (acknowledged, fix postponed to future research); *Proposed only* (mitigation exists solely as an unbuilt specification or "potential integration"); *Unacknowledged* (not recognized as a problem); *Contested* (framework openly disagrees with the critique); *Defect* (editorial/structural error).
- **Suggested Enhancement** — reserved for the next step. Do not populate during this review pass.

## Cross-Cutting Findings (context for the enhancement step)

1. **One missing piece dominates.** A majority of the Critical/High rows (B-1, B-2, B-4, C-6, D-1, D-4, D-10, G-1) reduce to the same absence: substantive priority, threshold, and override rules that convert reported findings into decisions. Fixing the conflict-resolution layer would discharge or downgrade many rows at once; enhancements should be sequenced accordingly.

3. **The concessions are an asset.** The document is unusually honest: nearly every weakness above is named somewhere within it (§9.2 responses, §9.3 open-issue lists, §9.4 limitations). 4. **Two traditions were engaged most successfully** (Stoicism via the dual-axis agent/transition split; Systems Ethics via the regulative reinterpretation of "completeness") — both because the framework openly disagreed or reframed rather than absorbing-by-proposal. 5. **Traditions not yet engaged.** Moral particularism, principlism (Beauchamp–Childress), divine command theory, egoism, and moral relativism/anti-realism receive no dedicated treatment in §9.2 or §9.3. Given the framework's legal/medical/governance application claims, principlism and particularism are the most consequential omissions. *(Queued as Phase 3 of the Recommended Remediation Sequence below.)*

## Recommended Remediation Sequence

The 50 weaknesses above are not independent: many cannot be meaningfully addressed until an upstream decision is made, and several would be discharged automatically (or downgraded in severity) by fixing an upstream item. The dependency analysis below drives the recommended sequence.

### Dependency Analysis

Four dependency chains structure the work:

1. **Identity gates requirement.** Until the framework decides what it *is* (A-4) and what "incorporated" obligates a conforming implementation to enforce (A-5), no constraint layer (Group D) can be specified as binding rather than decorative, and the defense of the axiom (A-1, A-2, A-3) has no fixed target. Everything normative is downstream of Phase 1.
2. **Ontology gates thresholds.** The materiality threshold τ (B-4) cannot be defined until it is settled *who counts* (C-1, C-2) and *what flourishing is* operationally (G-3, which subsumes the capability-dimension problem G-1). Ecosystem and abiotic standing (C-3, C-4) must be decided before environmental priority rules (C-6) can bind anything.
3. **Constraint layers gate conflict resolution.** The conflict-resolution procedure (B-1) resolves conflicts *among layers* — deontic constraints (D-1, D-10), rights (D-4), materiality (B-4), care/relational obligations (D-7–D-9), and horizon-weighted evidence (E-3). Building B-1 before those layers exist would formalize the resolution of undefined inputs. B-2 and B-3 (output determinacy) are consequences of B-1 and cannot be fixed independently.
4. **Validation comes last, but its harness is built early.** Independent validation (H-1) and implementation-consistency testing (E-5) can only validate built artifacts (H-5). However, the eight Example Assessments in §8 should be adopted as the standing regression suite from Phase 2 onward: every phase's output should be re-run against them.

Two tracks are genuinely parallelizable: the **epistemics track** (Phase 5) has no dependency on the constraint layers and can proceed alongside Phase 4; the **editorial track** (Phase 0) has no dependencies at all.

- **Regression discipline.** From Phase 2 onward, every phase's output should be re-run against the eight Example Assessments (§8, lines 10159–11696) and any changed verdict explained.
